## Format
Short romantic slice-of-life, **no dialogue, no subtitles, SFX only**, K-drama style.
Girl + white cat that secretly turns into a human. Same lived-in world every episode
(cluttered pink bedroom, small night kitchen). Episodes ~55s–1:28.

## THE #1 LESSON (their words)
> Every character, prop, and room is locked to an actual reference photo, tagged
> @image1…@Image15, pulled by tag into any prompt that needs it, **with an explicit
> instruction never to re-describe or expand the environment beyond what the photo
> already shows.**
>
> "This turned out to be the single most important lock in the whole project — the
> model's own instinct to *helpfully* widen a room or add furniture it wasn't shown
> was the most consistent source of drift between clips, **more than anything to do
> with the characters themselves**."

→ So: an ENVIRONMENT/SET lock matters MORE than the character lock. We did not have this.

## Their CONSTRAINTS block (verbatim shape — reusable)
> face and clothing consistency to reference at all times; natural smooth movements,
> no floating or stiff posing; no blurry motion, no morphing or liquid transitions on
> solid surfaces; lighting plain and motivated by ONE identifiable source per shot, no
> decorative sunbeams, no unearned golden-hour glow; facial expressions realistic and
> naturalistic, never exaggerated or theatrical; cat-to-man transformation never shown
> mid-morph unless staged as a shadow silhouette — otherwise off-screen, only
> before/after shown. No subtitles. No dialogue. SFX only.

## Transferable rules
- Lock the SET as hard as the cast — add "no new furniture/walls/layout" to every prompt
- One identifiable light source per shot; ban decorative sunbeams + unearned golden hour
- Ban mid-morph transformations; stage as silhouette or cut around it
- Day/night variants of each room are SEPARATE references
- No-dialogue + SFX-only sidesteps lip-sync entirely — huge for speed
- Sound design treated as a storytelling tool, manually cleaned per track
